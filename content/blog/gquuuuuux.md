# Mobile Suit Gundam GQuuuuuuX - A Love-Hate Relationship

I just finished Mobile Suit Gundam GQuuuuuuX and have really mixed feelings about the series. I had previously watched MSG: The Witch from Mercury and fell in love with the series. I built the HG Aerial kit, bought a Hi-Nu Gundam kit, and bought the enlish release of Beltorchika's Children all within a few weeks of watching WfM. I knew GQuuuuuuX was similarly an alternate universe story so it was intended to be standalone from the main timeline and main story ... or so I thought. In reality, the show really tries to pander to both old and new fans alike while failing both in the process.

## The Good

GQuuuuuuX did several things right. The animation was stellar. From fluid mobile suit movement to 70's acid trip space magic, the animation team did a wonderful job bringing this story to life and making it a visual spectacle. The characters all look good and stand out, especially Machu with her colorful hair and highly emotive expressions. The colors pop; the movement dazzles. The show visually is pretty stunning. Mobile Suits move nimbly and at the same time demonstrate weight, inertia, and energy. The designs of the mobile suits are also quite striking. The re-imagining of the RX-78 as the Red Gundam retains a lot of the vintage charm while showcasing a highly futuristic and more modern design. The soundtrack is also superb. While the opening isn't my favorite, the backround tracks and the main ED were all very good and thematic.

## The Bad

Even from limited experience, I can tell that Mobile Suit Gundam stories benefit from a longer series. They need time in the oven to really give room for the intricate plots to breathe. The standard twelve episode seasonal anime release just does not work well with Gundam plots. GQuuuuuuX had to do in twelve episodes what WfM did in 25, and I still feel like WfM could have used a little more time to develop some plot points. For GQX to tackle its plot in just 12 episodes was an insanely tall order and not attainable. They missed the mark and ended up not really making an impact for me. Part of the problem lies in the settings. GQX is set in an alternate reality that takes place shortly after the events of the original Gundam series, 0079. In this version, Zeon wins the war and largely controls space. Char Aznable pilots the Gundam and changes it to the Red Gundam, his personal battle colors. While this is an interesting angle and setting, the writers did not set it up well and did not introduce it well to the audience. A large chunk of the problem is due to how the show aired: it originally aired as a intro movie of the first two or three episodes and then the 8th (episodes 2 and 8 are both flashbacks). This would have helped properly set up the setting for the audience instead of relying on them understanding the events of 0079. The show abrubtly introduces the main characters: Machu, Nyaan, and Shuji without giving time to get invested into their characters. Machu's personality is the only one strong enough to make an impact. Shuji and Nyaan both feel like afterthoughts until the last two or three epsiodes where they suddenly become important.

## The Dissapointing

Overall, GQuuuuuuX was just whelming. The good animation, music, and some good characters felt wasted on a show that couldn't decide if it wanted to be a weird trippy "what-if" scenario based on the original show or if it wanted to be its own thing. It wasn't until the last few episodes where it really felt solidified in one direction. Even then, because it took so long to decide what it was, the ending lacked any impact because there was no reason to get invested. The show constantly references characters, places, and things from the original series that don't have any impact whatsoever. Classic mobile suit designs are shown and then immediately destroyed, while the new designs take center stage. But then entire episodes are dedicated to original characters a new viewer would have no understanding or feelings for. The identity of the show is stuck between the newer style shows and a weird homage to the original series. It feels like the show panders to new viewers by dangling these new, modern designs in front of the viewer just to shove a weird retelling/homage/AU of the original series down your throat. It genuinely tries to do too much for a series that traditionally has had twice the episodes to tell its story.
